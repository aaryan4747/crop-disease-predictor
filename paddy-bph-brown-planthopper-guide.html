Management of Brown Planthopper (BPH) & Hopperburn in Paddy Rice Fields

Published by ICAR-IIRR Rice Research Directorate • Category: Cereal Pathology • 6 min read

The Brown Planthopper (Nilaparvata lugens Stål) is among the most damaging insect pests in rice agro-ecosystems across India. Both nymphs and adults cluster at the base of rice tillers, sucking sap from phloem tissues. Intense feeding causes drying, yellowing, and rapid browning of entire circular field patches, a destructive condition known as "Hopperburn".

1. Recognizing Hopperburn & Early Infestation

Farmers frequently miss initial BPH infestations because insects congregate near water level at the base of dense rice clumps:

  • Base Congregation: Tiny light-to-dark brown insects (2–4 mm long) congregating on lower stems 2 to 4 inches above the water line.
  • Circular Yellowing Patches: Circular patches of plants turning yellow, rapidly drying to brownish straw color, looking as if scorched by fire.
  • Honeydew & Sooty Mold: Excreted honeydew coats stems and leaves, triggering secondary black sooty mold fungus that blocks photosynthesis.

2. Economic Threshold Level (ETL)

Scout 20 random hills across the field by gently tapping stems over a palm or white paper sheet:

  • Tillering Stage: 5 to 10 insects per hill.
  • Flowering / Panicle Stage: 15 to 20 insects per hill.

3. ICAR & CIBRC Approved Chemical Dosages

🧪 Certified Spray Recommendations (per Liter of Water):

Note: Direct spray nozzles strictly toward the plant base where insects shelter.

  • Triflumezopyrim 10% SC (Dupont / Corteva Pexalon): 0.5 ml per Liter of water (94 ml/acre in 200L water). Provides immediate feeding cessation and 21-day residual control. PHI: 21 Days.
  • Pymetrozine 50% WDG (Chess / Plastico): 0.6 grams per Liter of water (120g/acre in 200L water). PHI: 19 Days.
  • Dinotefuran 20% SG (Token / Oshin): 0.4 grams per Liter of water (80–100g/acre in 200L water). PHI: 14 Days.

4. Cultural & Water Management Techniques

  • Alternate Wetting & Drying (AWD): Drain stagnant field water for 3 to 4 days during early infestation to drastically reduce egg hatching and nymph survival.
  • Provide "Alley Ways" (Skipped Rows): Leave a 30 cm alleyway (passage) every 2 meters during transplanting to allow sunlight and aeration into the crop canopy.
  • Avoid Indiscriminate Synthetic Pyrethroids: Synthetic pyrethroid sprays (e.g. Cypermethrin) kill natural predators (mirid bugs and spiders) and trigger severe BPH pest resurgence.

Standardized reference: ICAR-Indian Institute of Rice Research (IIRR), Hyderabad. Always follow certified label instructions before field chemical applications.
