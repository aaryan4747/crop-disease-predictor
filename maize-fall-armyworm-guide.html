Maize Fall Armyworm (Spodoptera frugiperda): Field Identification, Whorl Application & Control

Compiled by the Crop Care AI editorial team from public ICAR and state agricultural university advisories • Category: Grain Crop Pathology • 7 min read

The invasive Fall Armyworm (Spodoptera frugiperda J.E. Smith) is a highly destructive polyphagous pest capable of devastating maize (corn) fields within days. Caterpillars feed voraciously inside the central leaf whorls and burrow into developing cobs, causing skeletonized leaves and sawdust-like fecal deposits that destroy photosynthesis and grain formation.

1. Identifying Fall Armyworm vs Regular Caterpillars

Correct identification of the larval stage is essential for timely control:

  • Inverted "Y" on Head: The dark head capsule bears a prominent, pale inverted "Y" shaped marking between the eyes.
  • Four Dark Dots on Eighth Abdominal Segment: The second-to-last body segment exhibits four distinct dark raised tubercles arranged in a precise square pattern.
  • "Shot-Hole" Whorl Damage: Young leaves emerging from the central whorl show ragged holes and parallel window-pane scrape marks.
  • Sawdust-Like Frass: Heavy accumulations of coarse, wet brown fecal pellets clogging the central whorl.

2. Economic Threshold Level (ETL)

  • Seedling to Knee-High Stage (up to 30 DAS): 5% to 10% damaged plants.
  • Mid-Whorl to Tasseling Stage: 10% to 20% damaged whorls.

3. Commonly Recommended Chemical Dosages

🧪 Commonly Used Spray Recommendations (Direct Nozzle into Whorl):

  • Chlorantraniliprole 18.5% SC (Coragen): 0.4 ml per Liter of water (80 ml/acre in 200L water). Delivers exceptional residual stomach poison efficacy. PHI: 14 Days.
  • Spinetoram 11.7% SC (Delegate): 0.5 ml per Liter of water (100 ml/acre in 200L water). PHI: 10 Days.
  • Emamectin Benzoate 5% SG (Proclaim): 0.4 grams per Liter of water (80g/acre in 200L water). PHI: 14 Days.

4. Biological IPM & Sand-Ash Whorl Application

  • Poison Baiting (Late Stage Caterpillars): Mix 10 kg rice bran + 1 kg jaggery + 100g Thiodicarb 75% WP or 100ml Chlorpyrifos in 2–3 liters water. Ferment for 24 hours and distribute small pellets into central whorls during the evening.
  • Sand-Lime Whorl Application: Mix dry sand or sieved wood ash with lime (9:1 ratio) and drop small pinches into whorls. The abrasive particles desiccate and kill caterpillars organically.
  • Egg Parasitoid Release: Release Trichogramma pretiosum or T. chilonis cards @ 50,000 eggs/acre at weekly intervals during early crop growth.

Reference source: ICAR-Indian Institute of Maize Research (IIMR), Ludhiana. Always wear safety gear during evening spray operations.
