Safe Pesticide Spraying: How to Read Labels, Protect Yourself and Avoid Residues

Compiled by the Crop Care AI editorial team from public ICAR and state agricultural university advisories • Category: Farm Safety • 8 min read

Most spray accidents and most residue problems come from the same mistakes: using the wrong dose, spraying too close to harvest, and skipping protection. This guide explains the basics in plain language. It applies to every crop guide on this site.

1. Read the label first

  • Crop and pest: use a product only on crops and pests listed on its label. If the crop is not listed, do not use it.
  • Dose: the label gives the amount per acre or per Litre. Using more does not give better control; it increases cost, resistance and residue.
  • Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI): the minimum number of days between the last spray and harvest. Never harvest earlier.
  • Colour triangle: the coloured band at the bottom of the label shows the toxicity class. Red and yellow products need extra care.
  • Registration: buy only from licensed dealers, keep the bill, and check that the product is registered for your crop.

2. Protective equipment

  • Wear: a full-sleeve shirt, long trousers, rubber gloves, rubber boots, a face mask or respirator, and eye protection or a cap with a visor.
  • Do not: eat, drink, smoke or touch your face while mixing or spraying.
  • After spraying: bathe with soap and wash the clothes separately before reuse.

3. Mixing and calibration

  • Use clean water: mix in a bucket first, then pour into the tank; measure with a marked cup, not by guess.
  • Do not mix products at random: some combinations damage crops or reduce effect. Mix only combinations recommended on labels or by your extension officer, and test a small quantity first.
  • Calibrate the sprayer: measure how much water your sprayer uses per acre, so the dose per tank is correct.
  • Nozzle: use the right nozzle and keep it clean; never blow into a blocked nozzle by mouth.

4. When and how to spray

  • Time: spray in the early morning or late evening when wind is low and bees are not active.
  • Wind: spray with the wind at your back and never against it.
  • Rain: avoid spraying if rain is expected within a few hours, as the product will wash off.
  • Reentry: keep people and animals out of the sprayed field until the spray has dried.

5. Storage and disposal

  • Store: in original, labelled containers, locked away from children, food and animal feed.
  • Empty containers: rinse three times, pour the rinse water into the spray tank, puncture the container and do not reuse it for water or food.
  • Leftover spray: do not pour it into ponds, canals or wells.

6. If someone feels unwell after exposure

Move the person away from the area, remove contaminated clothes, wash skin with plenty of water and soap, and rinse eyes with clean water for 15 minutes. If the person has breathing difficulty, vomiting, headache, sweating or confusion, take them to the nearest hospital immediately and carry the product label. You can call 112 for emergency help, or the National Poisons Information Centre helpline (1800-116-117). Do not induce vomiting unless a doctor advises it.
