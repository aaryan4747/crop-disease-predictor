Management of Banana Sigatoka Leaf Spot & Panama Wilt (Fusarium)

Compiled by the Crop Care AI editorial team from public ICAR and state agricultural university advisories • Category: Fruit Crop Pathology • 6 min read

Banana plantations across Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Maharashtra, and Kerala frequently face twin fungal threats: foliar Sigatoka Leaf Spot (Pseudocercospora musae / P. fijiensis) and soil-borne vascular Panama Wilt (Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. cubense). Both diseases cause premature leaf death, undersized fruit bunches, and catastrophic tree collapse if unaddressed.

1. Identifying Sigatoka vs Panama Wilt Symptoms

  • Sigatoka Leaf Spot: Tiny yellowish-green streaks appear parallel to secondary veins on the third and fourth leaves. Streaks expand into spindle-shaped brown lesions with gray necrotic centers and dark reddish halos. Severe infection causes entire leaves to turn brown and scorch prematurely.
  • Panama Wilt (Fusarium): Lower older leaves turn yellow starting from leaf margins towards the midrib. The petiole buckles at the pseudostem junction, creating a distinctive "skirt" of hanging dead leaves. Longitudinal splitting of the pseudostem base reveals reddish-brown vascular discolored strands.

2. Commonly Recommended Fungicide Dosages

🧪 Commonly Used Spray Recommendations for Sigatoka (per Liter of Water):

  • Propiconazole 25% EC (Tilt): 1.0 ml per Liter of water + 10 ml mineral oil / sticker. Apply when weather is overcast and humid. PHI: 15 Days.
  • Azoxystrobin 18.2% + Difenoconazole 11.4% SC (Amistar Top): 1.0 ml per Liter of water. PHI: 14 Days.
  • Carbendazim 50% WP (Bavistin): 1.0 gram per Liter of water. Rotate systemic fungicides to prevent pathogen resistance. PHI: 15 Days.

3. Soil Management for Panama Wilt & Sucker Treatment

  • Corm & Sucker Dip: Pare diseased roots from suckers and dip in a 0.2% Carbendazim solution (2g/L water) for 20 minutes prior to planting.
  • Bio-Agent Soil Drench: Apply Pseudomonas fluorescens or Trichoderma viride (50g per plant) mixed in 5 kg neem cake and compost at planting and at 3rd, 5th, and 7th months after planting.
  • De-leafing Sanitation: Promptly cut and remove heavily infected lower leaves displaying dried Sigatoka spots to remove spore inoculum from the plantation.

Reference source: ICAR-National Research Centre for Banana (NRCB), Tiruchirappalli. Always maintain proper drainage to avoid root hypoxia and Fusarium proliferation.
