Sugarcane Red Rot (Colletotrichum falcatum): Comprehensive Field Agronomy Guide

Published by ICAR-SBI Sugarcane Breeding Institute • Category: Commercial Crop Pathology • 6 min read

Often termed the "Cancer of Sugarcane", Red Rot caused by the fungus Colletotrichum falcatum Went is the most devastating disease affecting sugarcane growers and sugar mills across Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, and Andhra Pradesh. It destroys internal cane parenchyma tissues, converts sucrose into glucose and alcohol, and can ruin entire ratoons if left unmanaged.

1. Diagnostic Symptoms in Standing Crops

Red rot typically manifests prominently 4 to 6 months after planting during monsoon humidity:

  • Foliage Yellowing & Wither: The third and fourth leaves from the cane crown show yellowing, drying along the margins, and eventual drooping.
  • Internal Crimson Red Discoloration with White Cross Bands: When an infected stalk is split longitudinally, the internal pith exhibits deep red vascular strands interrupted by characteristic horizontal white patches or transverse bands across the pith.
  • Alcoholic Acidic Odor: Splitting the cane releases an immediate, sour alcoholic fermentation smell.
  • Midrib Lesions: Small red lesions appear on the upper midrib of leaves, developing into long dark red blood-colored stripes with tiny black fruiting pycnidia.

2. Mandatory Seed Sett Treatment & Chemical Dips

🧪 Certified Pre-Planting Sett Treatment:

  • Carbendazim 50% WP (Bavistin): Dip 2-budded or 3-budded setts in a 0.1% solution (1.0 gram per Liter of water) for 15 to 20 minutes before planting.
  • Thiophanate Methyl 70% WP (Roko): 1.5 grams per Liter of water dip for 15 minutes.
  • Hot Water / Aerated Steam Treatment: Treat seed cane at 50°C for 2 hours or 52°C for 30 minutes in specialized seed nursery facilities to eradicate internal latent mycelium.

3. Biological & Cultural Management Practices

  • Soil Biocontrol: Apply Trichoderma harzianum or T. viride enriched with well-rotted Farm Yard Manure (FYM @ 2.5 kg/acre) in furrows at planting.
  • Eradicate Ratoon of Infected Fields: Never harvest ratoon crops from plots affected by Red Rot. Ploug down and burn stubble after harvest.
  • Field Drainage: Ensure rapid surface drainage. Stagnant flood water spreads conidia from infected plants to healthy rows throughout the monsoon.

Standardized reference: ICAR-Sugarcane Breeding Institute (SBI), Coimbatore. Consult local mill extension staff for disease-resistant cane varieties (e.g. Co 0238 substitutes).
