Tomato Leaf Curl Virus (ToLCV) & Whitefly Management: Protecting the Crop in the First 45 Days

Compiled by the Crop Care AI editorial team from public ICAR and state agricultural university advisories • Category: Vegetable Pathology • 7 min read

Tomato Leaf Curl Virus (a Begomovirus) is one of the most damaging problems for tomato growers in Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu and Maharashtra. The virus is carried from plant to plant by the whitefly Bemisia tabaci. When plants are infected in the nursery or in the first month after transplanting, yield loss can be very high, because the plant stays small and sets very few fruits.

1. How to recognise leaf curl

  • Curled, crinkled leaves: young leaves become small, thick and curl upward like a cup, with yellow margins.
  • Stunted, bushy plants: shortened internodes give the plant a compact, stunted look.
  • Poor fruit set: flowers drop and the few fruits that form are small and hard.
  • Whiteflies on the underside of leaves: shake a branch gently; tiny white insects fly up. Heavy whitefly counts in hot, dry weather usually come before the disease appears.

Leaf curl is sometimes confused with herbicide drift, nutrient deficiency or mite damage. Check the underside of the leaves for whiteflies and look at whether the symptoms start on new growth, which points towards the virus.

2. The most important fact: there is no cure

Fungicides and antibiotics do not work on a virus. Once a plant is infected, no spray will reverse it. The whole strategy is therefore prevention: keep whiteflies off young plants and remove infected plants early.

3. Prevention that works

  • Raise seedlings under a nylon net: use a 40 to 50 mesh insect-proof net over the nursery bed so whiteflies cannot reach the seedlings.
  • Choose tolerant hybrids: ask a reliable seed supplier or your KVK which hybrids are reported as tolerant to leaf curl in your area.
  • Rogue out infected plants: pull up plants showing leaf curl in the first weeks and bury them away from the field.
  • Yellow sticky traps: install about 10 to 12 traps per acre at canopy height to monitor and reduce adult whiteflies.
  • Border crops and weed control: two or three rows of maize or sorghum around the plot act as a barrier, and clean field bunds remove alternate hosts.
  • Silver reflective mulch: reflective mulch repels whiteflies and also conserves soil moisture.

4. Whitefly control: commonly used options

Commonly used whitefly sprays (per Litre of water)

Rotate between different chemical groups and avoid using the same group more than twice in a season to slow resistance. Avoid spraying neonicotinoids while the crop is in full flower, because they are toxic to bees.

  • Imidacloprid 17.8% SL: 0.3 to 0.5 ml per Litre, mainly for the nursery and early stage.
  • Thiamethoxam 25% WG: 0.3 g per Litre.
  • Spiromesifen 22.9% SC: 1.0 ml per Litre.
  • Diafenthiuron 50% WP: 1.0 g per Litre.
  • Neem oil (1500 ppm): about 3 ml per Litre with a sticker, used as a repellent between chemical sprays.

Read the label for the correct crop, dose and the pre-harvest interval before every spray.
