Brinjal (Eggplant) Shoot & Fruit Borer: Field Identification and Integrated Control

Compiled by the Crop Care AI editorial team from public ICAR and state agricultural university advisories • Category: Vegetable Pathology • 6 min read

The Brinjal Shoot and Fruit Borer (Leucinodes orbonalis) is the most serious pest of brinjal across India. The caterpillar bores into growing shoots in the early stage and into fruits later, making them unfit for sale. Because the larva feeds from inside the plant, sprays alone often fail. Success depends on timing, sanitation and rotating control methods.

1. Symptoms

  • Drooping shoot tips: in young plants, the tip of a shoot wilts and dries because the larva has tunnelled inside.
  • Bore holes on fruits: small entry holes plugged with excreta (frass), often near the fruit stalk or calyx.
  • Damaged fruit: tunnels inside the flesh and discoloured, deformed fruits that rot quickly.

2. Scouting

Check 20 plants at random every week. Count wilted shoots and bored fruits. Begin control as soon as damage is first seen in the shoots, because that is when the population is still low.

3. Non-chemical measures that reduce the pest

  • Clip and destroy wilted shoots: remove drooping shoot tips weekly and bury or burn them with the larva inside.
  • Collect damaged fruits: do not leave bored fruits in the field; remove and destroy them at every picking.
  • Pheromone traps: install about 5 traps per acre with the species-specific lure and change the lure as per the manufacturer's schedule.
  • Egg parasitoid: release Trichogramma chilonis at roughly 50,000 per acre at weekly intervals as per local KVK advice.
  • Neem: spray 5% Neem Seed Kernel Extract as a deterrent in the early stage.

4. Commonly used insecticides

Commonly used sprays (per Litre of water)

Pick fruits before spraying, rotate between groups, and do not spray more often than the label allows. Always follow the label for waiting period.

  • Emamectin benzoate 5% SG: 0.4 g per Litre.
  • Chlorantraniliprole 18.5% SC: 0.3 ml per Litre.
  • Spinosad 45% SC: 0.3 ml per Litre.

Avoid repeated use of synthetic pyrethroids. They kill natural enemies and the borer population often rebounds stronger.
