Cotton Pink Bollworm (Pectinophora gossypiella): Complete Field Management Guide

Published by ICAR-CICR Cotton Agronomy Panel • Category: Fiber Crop Pathology • 7 min read

The Pink Bollworm (Pectinophora gossypiella Saunders) has emerged as the most critical pest threatening Bt-cotton cultivation across Maharashtra, Gujarat, Telangana, and Andhra Pradesh. Larvae burrow directly into developing cotton bolls, feeding on seeds and staining lint, leading to "rosetted flowers", premature boll opening, and severe reductions in commercial lint quality.

1. Identifying Infestation Symptoms in the Field

Early field scouting is essential because pink bollworm caterpillars remain hidden inside flowers and green bolls:

  • Rosetted Flowers: Webbed petals that fail to open normally, resembling a rosette or closed rosebud. Inside, young caterpillars feed on anthers and staminal columns.
  • Bore Holes in Developing Bolls: Microscopic entry holes on green bolls that heal rapidly, leaving small brown spots. Infested bolls rot internally and produce discolored, unmarketable lint.
  • Premature Dropping: Young squares and bolls drop prematurely during peak vegetative and flowering stages.

2. Economic Threshold Level (ETL) Monitoring

Chemical intervention should strictly be guided by ETL metrics to prevent chemical resistance:

  • 8 to 10 adult male moths trapped per pheromone trap per night for 3 consecutive nights.
  • 10% rosetted flowers or 10% damaged green bolls (examined by dissecting 20 random green bolls per acre).

3. ICAR & CIBRC Approved Chemical Spray Dosages

🧪 Certified Spray Recommendations (per Liter of Water):

  • Emamectin Benzoate 5% SG (Proclaim): 0.45 grams per Liter of water (80g/acre in 200L water). PHI: 14 Days.
  • Chlorantraniliprole 18.5% SC (Coragen): 0.3 ml per Liter of water (60ml/acre in 200L water). PHI: 21 Days.
  • Profenofos 50% EC: 2.0 ml per Liter of water. Apply during evening hours for ovicidal action against eggs. PHI: 15 Days.

4. Integrated Pest Management (IPM) & Cultural Practices

  • Pheromone Traps: Install 5 to 8 sleeve pheromone traps with Gossyplure septa per acre at 45 days after sowing (DAS) to detect moth emergence.
  • Crop Residue Destruction: Immediately after the final picking, shred and bury cotton stalks to terminate diapausing larvae. Avoid ratoon cotton cultivation.
  • Light Traps: Operate solar light traps between 7:00 PM and 10:00 PM to capture nocturnal adult moths.

Standardized reference: ICAR-Central Institute for Cotton Research (CICR) & CIBRC insecticide guidelines. Always consult your district Agricultural Extension Officer before large-scale pesticide spraying.
